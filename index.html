Two numbers out of nine

The 2026 Match recorded nine measures for every non-U.S. IMG: an applicant who went to medical school outside the United States and is not a U.S. citizen. Two of those measures told the applicants who matched apart from those who did not. The other seven came out identical.

A note on the 41.5% you'll see quoted elsewhere

The NRMP report leads with "41.5 percent of non-U.S. IMGs matched to their preferred specialty." That figure is the unweighted average of the 24 individual specialty rates, so it counts Vascular Surgery, with 28 IMG applicants, the same as Internal Medicine, with 6,992.

The applicant-weighted rate, which is the number that describes an actual person's odds, is 49.0% (5,721 matched of 11,685). This site uses the weighted figure throughout and shows the underlying counts so you can check it.

Where the pressure comes from

Each circle is a specialty, sized by how many non-U.S. IMGs ranked it first, and the horizontal axis is total applicants per available position, which measures how contested that specialty is overall.

Overall pressure predicts IMG outcomes

The correlation between all-applicants-per-position and IMG match rate is −0.84. The correlation between IMG density and IMG match rate is +0.19, which is essentially nothing.

Pathology has the second-highest IMG density of any specialty and a workable 45.1% rate. Dermatology has almost no IMG applicants and a 16.7% rate.

Family Medicine breaks the pattern

FM was the least subscribed specialty in the Match, with 5,491 positions against 4,685 applicants preferring it, and yet 513 of the 1,112 non-U.S. IMGs who ranked it first still did not match into it.

Their Step 2 medians were identical to those who matched (230 vs 231). The only separator was contiguous ranks: 3 versus 1.

Concentration risk

Internal Medicine absorbs 59.8% of all non-U.S. IMG first choices, and the top three take 76.1%.

Pediatrics, the highest match rate in the entire report at 68.5%, draws only 6.8%.

What this data cannot tell you

Six limits that decide how far these numbers can be pushed.

Only Step 2 CK is verified

NRMP obtains Step 2 CK scores directly from the USMLE program. Everything else, from research and abstracts through publications, work, volunteering and degrees, is self-reported at registration and is neither verified nor quality-assessed.

The eight Neurosurgery applicants who matched report a median of 44 research experiences and 70 publications, with one applicant claiming 199. Orthopaedics shows a maximum of 493. Those figures are reporting artefacts, and nothing in the report checks them.

2026 breaks comparability

Abstracts, presentations and publications were a single combined item before 2026 and were split into three separate items this cycle. Do not compare these three against earlier editions of Charting Outcomes, which is the report NRMP publishes each year on how applicants who matched differed from those who did not.

This is also the first year non-U.S. IMGs were reported separately from U.S.-citizen IMGs, so prior "IMG" figures are not comparable either.

Contiguous ranks mostly measure interview volume

You cannot rank a program that did not interview you. A long run of ranks inside one specialty largely reflects how many interviews an applicant received, so it works as a symptom of a strong application rather than a lever you pull directly.

The useful signal is the unmatched median of 1 to 2. A large share of unmatched IMGs got only one or two interviews, which puts the failure earlier in the cycle than the rank list.

Distributions overlap heavily

Matched Internal Medicine applicants ranged from 209 to 284 on Step 2 CK, and unmatched ones reached 279, so a high score does not secure a match and a modest one does not disqualify you. The overlap is nearly total.

A median describes a group and forecasts nothing about an individual applicant.

The biggest variables are missing

Visa status, year of graduation, U.S. clinical experience, letters of recommendation, and the MSPE (the Medical Student Performance Evaluation, or dean's letter) are plausibly the strongest determinants for non-U.S. IMGs. None of them appear anywhere in this report.

NRMP itself notes that course evaluations, reference letters and the MSPE also contribute to outcomes.

Who is counted

Only applicants who certified a rank order list and consented to research use of their data. Anyone who never reached the ranking stage is invisible here, and that is where a great many IMG applications actually end.

Applicants who ranked a preliminary or transitional-year program first are excluded, as are specialties offering fewer than 50 positions.
