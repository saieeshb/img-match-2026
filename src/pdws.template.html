The screen on the other side

The rest of this site measures what happened after programs chose who to look at. This page is that choosing. It recreates the Program Director's WorkStation, the AAMC software a residency program uses to filter, sort and open the applications it receives.

It is a demonstration program filled with 732 invented applicants. The filters run for real, so every count on it is computed. Open Manage Filters to read what a program can select on before anyone opens a personal statement: country of medical school, LCME accreditation, visa sponsorship, ECFMG certification, number of exam attempts. Build one yourself in New Criteria and the line under the builder says how many of the 732 come through it.

Turn on applicant notes and the fields that decide most for a non-U.S. IMG pick up a line of commentary. Leave them off and what is left is the interface.

What is real here and what is not

The interface is a recreation. It carries no AAMC logo or branding, it is not connected to any AAMC system, and it is not the product. Category and field names shown are the ones the criteria builder marks as observed; the rest are reconstructed from what ERAS collects, and the builder says so underneath whenever you are looking at one.

Every applicant is invented. Names, AAMC IDs, scores, dates and document states come from a seeded random generator, and the mix of applicant types is chosen to resemble a large Internal Medicine pool rather than measured from one. Medical school names are real institutions, because a filter on medical school means nothing without them, and no record here belongs to anyone who attended one.

Sections that are not part of the demonstration are not filled in. Rankings, Reports, Archives and the rest say so when you open them.

Move through it the way a program would: pick a filter, sort a column, open a row. Applications ▾ → Manage Filters
